## Action

The cat noses a wedge of cheese to the base of an upright box, then shoves its muzzle under the box's edge and heaves the box up until its raised corner rests on the cheese. The cat pads left, leaps onto a low stump and crouches there, watching. A mouse pops out of its hole, sniffs the cheese, and drags it away; robbed of its prop the box starts to topple, and the cat springs off the stump. The box slams down over the cat, leaving only its hindquarters and tail sticking out, while the mouse scurries back into its hole with the cheese.

## Notes

- Both animals stay strictly quadrupedal: they push, nose, drag and pounce with head, mouth, paws and body weight only.
- Fixed side view on a single ground line: box trap centre-right, stump and mouse hole lower left, open ground between them.
- Cat: outline quadruped with pointed ears, whiskers and a long curling tail; Mouse: small compact quadruped with large round ears, pointed nose and a long thin tail.
- The box is propped only by the cheese wedge, so pulling the cheese must visibly precede the box toppling.
